# The Candor-Culture Company — Behavioral Interview Prep

Read `FRAMEWORK.md` first for the STAR(L) structure and how to build your story bank —
this doc is just the mapping and mechanics for a company that runs on an explicit,
publicly-documented culture memo and evaluates you against it directly.

## How the round is actually run

Interviewers score you directly against this company's published culture values; a
**"keeper test"** framing is common internally ("would I fight to keep this person on the
team") even if not asked in those exact words. This kind of company is unusually explicit,
compared to others, that its culture document is not aspirational marketing but describes
how the company actually expects people to operate day to day — which means the interview
genuinely probes for it, not just a generically positive personality.

## Culture values (condensed)

Treat these as illustrative — this kind of published culture memo tends to get revised
over the years — but the underlying theme (direct, unusually candid, high individual
ownership) tends to stay consistent and is worth calibrating for specifically.

| Value | What it's really asking |
|---|---|
| Judgment | A time you made a good decision despite ambiguous or incomplete information. |
| Communication | A time you gave someone difficult feedback clearly and respectfully — and a time you *received* it well. |
| Curiosity | A time you learned something outside your role that changed how you approached a problem. |
| Courage | A time you said something unpopular because it was true, even at personal cost. |
| Passion | A time you pushed for a higher bar than what was asked of you. |
| Selflessness | A time you gave up credit, scope, or a preferred approach for the team's benefit. |
| Innovation | A time you reframed a problem instead of accepting the first framing given to you. |
| Inclusion | A time you actively brought in a perspective that would otherwise have been missing. |
| Integrity | A time you were transparent about a mistake or bad outcome before being asked. |
| Impact | A time your individual output was disproportionate to your title/level. |

## Company-specific failure mode

A conflict-avoidant or softened version of a hard-feedback story. Given how explicitly
the culture rewards directness, an answer that dodges the uncomfortable part of the story
— vague about what was actually said, or about how the other person actually reacted —
reads as a bigger red flag here than a generic "could be more specific" critique would be
elsewhere. The interviewer is listening for evidence you can actually do the uncomfortable
thing, not just that you know it's valued.

## Prep checklist specific to this loop

- Have a genuine, non-sanitized story for **both** "a time you gave tough feedback" and
  "a time you received tough feedback and changed your behavior" — these are close to
  mandatory here and having only one direction covered is a common gap.
- In the Action section of your feedback stories, include the actual words/substance of
  what was said, not just "I gave them feedback" — specificity is what separates a real
  answer from a hedge here.
- Have one "Courage" story that cost you something real (social capital, a relationship,
  short-term standing) — a Courage story with no real cost isn't really about courage.
