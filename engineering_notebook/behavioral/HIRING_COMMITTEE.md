# The Hiring-Committee Company — Behavioral Interview Prep

Read `FRAMEWORK.md` first for the STAR(L) structure and how to build your story bank —
this doc is just the mapping and mechanics for a company where a separate hiring
committee, not your interviewer, makes the final call.

## How the round is actually run

Structured behavioral interview(s) — commonly framed as assessing general leadership and
cultural fit — feed into a written packet reviewed by a **separate hiring committee**
that never met you. Your interviewer's written notes have to stand alone and be
convincing to people who weren't in the room, which means a story that only "landed"
because of your delivery (tone, charisma) loses most of its value once it's reduced to
notes — the content and structure have to carry it. General cognitive ability and
role-related knowledge (largely assessed elsewhere in the loop) get you to onsite; this
round meaningfully affects whether the committee extends an offer.

## Competency buckets

| Bucket | What it's really asking |
|---|---|
| General cognitive ability | Structured problem-solving under ambiguity — reinforced here, mostly assessed in technical rounds. |
| Leadership | Emergent leadership (influence without authority), weighted higher than formal management for IC tracks. |
| Role-related knowledge | Domain-specific judgment calls relevant to your discipline. |
| Cultural fit | Comfort with ambiguity, collaborative instinct, intellectual humility — a story where you changed your mind based on new evidence is strong signal here specifically. |

## Company-specific failure mode

A story that depends on being *told well* rather than being *inherently well-structured*.
Because your interviewer's written summary — not your live delivery — is what the hiring
committee actually reads, practice retelling each story as if you were writing the
committee notes yourself: does the Situation/Task/Action/Result survive being compressed
to four sentences by someone else? If a story only works with your specific phrasing or
tone, tighten the underlying structure, not just the delivery.

## Prep checklist specific to this loop

- For each story, write a one-paragraph "committee note" version (imagine your
  interviewer summarizing it for someone who wasn't there) and check it still reads as
  a clear demonstration of leadership or cultural fit on its own.
- Have at least one strong "changed my mind based on evidence" story ready — this is the
  cultural-fit dimension most candidates under-prepare for relative to leadership.
- Because role-related knowledge is also lightly assessed here, don't fully strip
  technical context out of your story — keep enough that your domain judgment is visible,
  while still keeping the focus on the decision/interpersonal texture.
