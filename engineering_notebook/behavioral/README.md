# Behavioral / Leadership Principles Prep

A lightweight companion to `dsa_prep/`, `system_design/`, `system_design_practice/`, and
`lld/` — this folder is **not** a MkDocs site, just plain markdown you read directly. It's
a framework and a set of fillable templates, not pre-written stories: nobody but you knows
your actual work history, and a fabricated STAR story will fall apart under a follow-up
question in the room. Fill in the templates with your own material.

Company names are intentionally not used in these docs — each per-company doc is named
and organized by the **distinguishing mechanic** of that company's loop instead (how it's
graded, what's structurally unique), which is the part that actually changes how you
prepare. Map each doc to the specific company you're interviewing with yourself.

## Why this matters as much as DSA at the toughest bar

At senior/staff+ loops, the behavioral round is not a formality — it's graded with the
same rigor as the coding rounds, and a strong technical performance paired with a weak
behavioral round is a common rejection pattern, not a rare one. The failure mode isn't "I
don't have good stories" — it's "I have the stories but can't retrieve and structure them
under pressure, or in the specific shape a given company grades for." That's a
rehearsal-and-calibration problem, and that's what this folder is for.

The *content* of a good story barely changes across companies — the *mechanics* of how
it's evaluated do, and knowing the mechanics changes how you prepare, not just what you
say in the room. That's why this is organized as one framework doc plus one doc per
grading archetype, mirroring how `dsa_prep/PATTERN.md` and `lld/OOD_FRAMEWORK.md` work:
read the framework once, then treat each archetype doc as that framework applied to a
specific bar.

## How to use this

1. Read [`FRAMEWORK.md`](FRAMEWORK.md) first — the STAR(L) structure, how to build your
   story bank from a raw brain-dump, and the senior-vs-staff+ scope calibration that
   applies underneath every company's specific bar.
2. Read the doc for the grading archetype that matches whichever company you're actually
   interviewing with (table below) — how that company's round is structurally run, its
   values/LP table as prompts (not a checklist to write fake stories against), its most
   common failure mode, and a short prep checklist.
3. Fill in [`STORY_BANK_TEMPLATE.md`](STORY_BANK_TEMPLATE.md) with your own material,
   tagging each story against the values/LPs it can honestly answer.
4. Rehearse out loud against the coverage checklist in that template — including the
   archetype-specific rows — before a real loop.

## Grading archetypes covered

| Doc | Grading archetype | What's structurally different about this loop |
|---|---|---|
| [`LP_BAR_RAISER.md`](LP_BAR_RAISER.md) | Large published-principles list + dedicated bar raiser | A bar raiser grades against ~16 named principles independent of the hiring team's opinion. |
| [`HIRING_COMMITTEE.md`](HIRING_COMMITTEE.md) | Separate hiring committee | A committee that never met you reviews written notes — the story has to survive being summarized by someone else. |
| [`SCOPE_LEVELING.md`](SCOPE_LEVELING.md) | Scope-calibrated leveling | Behavioral scope is cross-checked against technical scope as a leveling signal (junior vs. senior vs. staff+). |
| [`CRAFT_AND_DISCRETION.md`](CRAFT_AND_DISCRETION.md) | Craft + compartmentalized org | Less standardized publicly; probes craftsmanship and collaboration across a need-to-know org. |
| [`CANDOR_CULTURE.md`](CANDOR_CULTURE.md) | Published culture-values memo | Scored directly against 9-10 published culture values; radical-candor feedback stories are close to mandatory. |
| [`WRITING_CULTURE.md`](WRITING_CULTURE.md) | Writing-heavy internal culture | Often includes an actual written component; structure is graded even in verbal answers. |

Treat every mechanic/value list in these docs as illustrative and approximate — companies
revise their interview processes and published values over time — but the *shape* of the
difference (who's grading, what they're allowed to see, what's uniquely probed) is worth
confirming for your specific loop via the recruiter, since it changes how you should
rehearse.

See [`FRAMEWORK.md`](FRAMEWORK.md) for the archetype-agnostic failure modes (rambling
without a Result, "we" language, etc.) that apply regardless of which company you're
prepping for.
