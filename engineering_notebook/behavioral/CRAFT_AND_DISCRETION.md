# The Craft & Discretion Company — Behavioral Interview Prep

Read `FRAMEWORK.md` first for the STAR(L) structure and how to build your story bank —
this doc is just the mapping and mechanics for a company that doesn't publish a
Leadership-Principles-style rubric and whose process is less publicly standardized than
the others covered here — treat everything below as directional, based on recurring
themes reported across loops, not a checklist to grade yourself against precisely.

## How the round is actually run

Typically several cross-functional interviewers (design, engineering, product) each
independently probe collaboration — worth noting because this kind of company's org is
often famously compartmentalized (need-to-know, even internally), so the interpersonal
challenge being tested is specifically collaborating productively *without* full
visibility into adjacent teams' work, which is a different texture than the "align three
teams" stories that work well elsewhere.

## Recurring themes

| Theme | What it's really asking |
|---|---|
| Craftsmanship / high polish bar | A time you kept refining something past "good enough" because the detail mattered — and a time you *didn't*, because you correctly judged polish wasn't the right call there. |
| Cross-functional collaboration under partial information | A time you aligned design, eng, and product despite not having the full picture. |
| Product/user sense | A time a technically-correct solution was still the wrong call from a user-experience standpoint, and you caught it. |
| Discretion | Comfort not discussing specifics of unreleased or confidential work, even when directly asked — a mild, implicit part of the evaluation. |

## Company-specific failure mode

A craftsmanship story that only shows *pursuing* polish, with no story showing you
correctly judged when *not* to. This kind of company's bar for detail is real, but an
answer that reads as "I always push for more polish, no matter what" misses the actual
judgment being tested — knowing when the marginal polish isn't worth the cost is as much
the signal as caring about it in the first place.

## Prep checklist specific to this loop

- Have one craftsmanship story where you pushed for more polish, and a *separate* one
  where you deliberately shipped something less polished because the tradeoff was right
  — both halves of the judgment matter here.
- For your cross-functional collaboration story, be specific about what you *didn't* have
  visibility into and how you worked around that, not just that multiple functions were
  involved.
- Don't over-index on quantified Results for every story here — a user-experience catch
  that's hard to quantify (e.g., "this would have confused first-time users") is still a
  strong answer in this context if the reasoning is sharp, even without a hard number.
