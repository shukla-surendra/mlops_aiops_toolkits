# The Scope-Leveling Company — Behavioral Interview Prep

Read `FRAMEWORK.md` first for the STAR(L) structure and how to build your story bank —
this doc is just the mapping and mechanics for a company that calibrates leveling partly
off behavioral scope, not just technical performance.

## How the round is actually run

Often paired with, or scheduled close in time to, a hiring-manager screen. Leveling
(junior IC vs. senior IC vs. staff+) is calibrated partly off behavioral scope — a
staff-scope technical answer paired with a junior-scope behavioral answer is a common and
specific rejection/downlevel pattern here, more so than at companies where leveling is
decided almost entirely off the technical bar.

## Competency buckets

| Bucket | What it's really asking |
|---|---|
| Execution speed | Shipped despite obstacles, cut scope deliberately rather than slipping the deadline. |
| Direction | Set or changed technical direction for a team/project, not just executed one. |
| Talent | Grew others, raised the bar for the people around you. |
| Tenacity | Pushed through a genuinely hard, unglamorous problem all the way to the end. |

## Company-specific failure mode

A behavioral answer that's scoped well below the level implied by your technical
performance. Interviewers here are explicitly cross-checking behavioral scope against
technical scope as a leveling signal — if your system design answer operates at a
cross-team/staff altitude but every behavioral story is "I personally fixed X," that
mismatch gets noticed and can cap your level even when the technical round was strong.

## Prep checklist specific to this loop

- Before the loop, explicitly check: does at least one story in your bank match the
  *scope* of your strongest technical answer? If your best system design story is
  staff-level, you need at least one staff-scope behavioral story too (see the
  senior-vs-staff calibration section in `FRAMEWORK.md`).
- Have a "Direction" story ready that isn't the same as your "Tenacity" story — these two
  buckets get conflated often (pushing hard vs. steering) and having genuinely distinct
  examples for each is a specific signal of range.
- If you're interviewing for a senior-staff-and-above level, prioritize "influence
  without authority" over "individual execution" stories — this bar weights that heavily
  above that level.
